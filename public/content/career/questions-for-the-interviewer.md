Interviews are best viewed as a negotiation. You're trying to find out whether the business and role are a good fit for you just as much as they're assessing you.

Asking questions helps you understand the role, and it also shows interest and that you're proactive.

## Questions worth asking

*"Do you have any feedback for me?"*
> A good question to always ask at the end. Whatever happened in the interview, feedback gives you takeaways for future interviews, and it shows you seek out and act on others' opinions.

*"What is your process for onboarding and training?"*
> Starting a new job can be daunting, especially remotely. This tells you what your first weeks look like and what's expected of you, and whether the company invests in people.

*"What is the work culture like?"*
> Listen for specifics rather than "we're like a family". Follow up: *"What did the team do together recently?"* or *"How are disagreements usually resolved?"*

*"What is the dress code?"*
> Practical, and it hints at formality and client-facing expectations.

*"What is the remote/hybrid working policy?"*
> Ask how it works *in practice*: which days people actually come in, and whether remote staff are first-class in meetings and promotions.

## About the engineering

- *"What does a typical week look like for someone in this role?"*
- *"How does code get from a laptop to production?"* (reveals CI/CD, review and testing culture)
- *"How do you handle on-call and incidents?"*
- *"How do you balance new features against tech debt?"*
- *"How is the team using AI tools, and what are the guidelines around them?"*

## About growth

- *"What would success look like in the first six months?"*
- *"How are progression and promotions decided?"*
- *"What's the biggest challenge the team faces right now?"*

> [!TIP]
> Prepare more questions than you need: some will be answered during the interview, and "No, I think you've covered everything" is a missed opportunity.

```recall
Q: Why should you always ask questions at the end of an interview?
A: The interview is two-way — you're assessing fit too — and thoughtful questions show interest and initiative.
```
